GUARDIAN: A One-Press Safety Kit for Women Built with Arduino, GPS and GSM

Our final-year capstone project at SR University, Warangal

The problem

According to the National Crime Records Bureau, more than 4.4 lakh cases of crime against women were registered in India in 2023. Behind each number is a moment when help was needed quickly. In that moment, unlocking a phone, opening an app and dialling someone can take too long. Many low-cost safety devices also depend on a smartphone, send only a single SMS, or send nothing when there is no GPS signal.

So we asked ourselves a simple question: can a woman call for help with just one press, without a phone and without the internet?

Our solution: GUARDIAN

GUARDIAN is a low-cost safety kit with its own SIM card and GPS receiver. It offers two kinds of alert.

Emergency alert. When the user presses the emergency button, a buzzer and LED switch on. An SMS reading "EMERGENCY ALERT! Help needed immediately." is sent with her location as a Google Maps link. The kit then automatically calls a family member.

Missing-kit alert. Each time the kit is switched on, the owner must press a confirmation button within 5 seconds. If no one does (for example, because the kit was lost or snatched), it sends a "MISSING KIT ALERT!" with its location and calls the contact.

If the GPS has no satellite signal, such as inside a building, the SMS still goes out with the note "GPS fix unavailable". The alert is never held back waiting for a location.

How it works

  1. The user presses the emergency button.
  2. The Arduino Uno switches on the buzzer and LED.
  3. The NEO-6M GPS module provides latitude and longitude.
  4. The SIM800A GSM modem sends the SMS with the Google Maps link.
  5. The modem then places a voice call to the family member.

Hardware and software

  • Arduino Uno R3: the controller that runs our firmware.
  • SIM800A GSM modem: sends the SMS and makes the call on the 2G network. It runs on its own 12 V adapter.
  • NEO-6M GPS module with an external patch antenna.
  • Two push buttons (emergency and confirmation) and a buzzer on a breadboard.
  • Firmware in Arduino C/C++, using the TinyGPS++ library and AT commands.

Results

In our demonstration, both the emergency alert and the missing-kit alert reached the registered phone, and each was followed by an automatic call. During an outdoor test, the SMS contained a Google Maps link (18.002853, 79.574478) that opened directly in Maps. Holding the button down sent only one alert, as designed.

What we learned

We learned how to connect GPS and GSM modules to a microcontroller and how to send SMS and calls with AT commands. We also learned why a GSM modem needs a power supply that can handle 2 A peaks, and how to test an embedded system step by step. Most of all, we learned how much careful design matters when a device is meant to help someone in danger.

What's next

We plan to build a small battery-powered wrist version, add live tracking and multiple contacts, and use on-device AI (TinyML) with a motion sensor to detect falls or struggles automatically.
